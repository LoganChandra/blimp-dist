# /decide — calibrated decision-making under uncertainty

Both agents make dozens of autonomous judgment calls per unattended run.
Without a shared protocol, calibration is vibes and escalate-vs-decide-alone
is inconsistent between Duke and Frank. This is that protocol.

## When to use

Any non-obvious choice you're about to make alone: which approach, whether
to spend the time, what to prioritize next, park-vs-push a venture lane.
**Not** for anything in the Boss-only band (money leaving, KYC, pricing/
refund/tier changes, legal posture, deletions) — those get packaged as ONE
askable line for the Boss, never decided solo regardless of how confident you
are.

## The procedure

1. **Classify the door.** Two-way door (cheap to reverse — decide now, log
   it) vs one-way door (expensive/impossible to reverse — escalate, or at
   minimum pre-register your success criteria before acting so a bad call
   is caught early, not after it's baked in).
2. **State the real options** — not a strawman vs the option you already
   want. For each, name the ONE discriminating question that would change
   your answer if you knew it.
3. **Attach calibrated confidence with numbers**, not vibes: "likely" =
   70-85%, "probably" = 55-70%, etc. — pick actual numbers, and state what
   evidence would move you off them. A confidence label with no attached
   "what would change my mind" isn't calibration, it's decoration.
4. **Pre-register the success check** before acting — what does "this
   worked" look like, checkable, not felt? This is what `foundry-verification`
   runs against later.
5. **Log the decision + confidence to the brain** (`remember(tags="decision,
   ..."`) so a future retro can score calibration — did "likely" outcomes
   actually land ~70-85% of the time? Compounding calibration only works if
   the predictions are written down before the outcome is known.

## Rules

- Two-way doors: decide and log. One-way doors: escalate, or pre-register
  hard before acting.
- A discriminating question you can't answer yet is a signal to gather that
  one piece of evidence before deciding, not to guess.
- Confidence talk without a "what would change my mind" is not calibration.
